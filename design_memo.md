# Design Memo — NimbusCare Policy-Grounded Workflow

This prototype uses a small hybrid pipeline: the offline language layer classifies a question and expands common billing synonyms; lexical retrieval ranks exact policy and knowledge-base passages; a deterministic engine selects the governing track, calculates amounts with `Decimal`, and applies account checks; a claim validator checks required source IDs and recomputes derived amounts before rendering. A chronological replay adds facts only after the transcript turn that reports them. Customer text remains data: it cannot change rules, calculations, escalation conditions, or perform an account action.

For Dana Whitfield's first question, Rule 3 is the specific service-outage policy. The transcript reports an agent NOC lookup and a $225 monthly plan charge for 11 full outage days, giving `(225 / 30) × 11 = $82.50`. Rule 3 and KB-1004 support the amount and its exemption from Rule 2's $75 cumulative goodwill limit. The ticket is labeled agent-reported because this offline replay has no independent NOC access. The source material does not establish credit history, a prior resolved dispute, or fraud status. The trace leaves repeat-dispute and fraud checks unknown, marks the amount pending those checks, and never says a credit was approved or issued.

For the tax question, KB-1004 grounds the statement that the outage credit applies to the recurring plan charge, while KB-1003 says taxes and surcharges are itemized separately. Neither source describes whether the billing system recalculates or refunds those items after a credit. The response states that limit, makes no yes/no tax claim, and escalates the unanswered portion. For the deadline question, Rule 3 and KB-1005 support a 30-calendar-day period after resolution. Given the transcript's September 21 resolution date, October 21 is the deadline; October 5 is timely.

The hardest rule-precedence case is keeping Rule 3 separate from the general Rule 2 cap while still applying cross-cutting Rule 4 and Rule 5 controls. A $82.50 outage calculation does not trigger Rule 2's $75 check. Rule 4's distinct outage thresholds—above $300, above 30 days, or an unverifiable ticket—and the repeat-dispute and fraud controls still matter. The rules JSON binds thresholds and named calculators to source sections; startup detects policy/KB or config/policy conflicts and blocks financial answers rather than blending conflicting values.

Evaluation should use versioned source documents and held-out supported, ambiguous, adversarial, paraphrased, and out-of-domain questions. Measure rule-selection accuracy, instruction-adherence rate, grounded-claim precision, unsupported consequential-claim rate, correct-escalation recall, unnecessary escalation, and answer coverage. A release gate should require zero unsupported consequential claims in targeted billing regressions; that is a goal, not an empirical claim. The bundled suite exercises the specified replay, threshold boundaries, source conflicts, unknown checks, malicious prompts, and non-billing KB topics.

The prototype has no live account, billing, or NOC integration, performs no credit issuance, and does not require an LLM. Its language interpretation and keyword retrieval are intentionally lightweight; unfamiliar requests are clarified or declined instead of answered from invented policy.
